A modem turns bits into radio and radio into bits

Modem means modulator plus demodulator. A cellular modem also runs the whole conversation with the network, so it is a radio, a signal processor and a protocol engine in one package.

Application processorApps, OS, IP stack. Hands packets to the modem over PCIe, USB or shared memory.
Layer 3RRC and NAS: connection control, registration, bearers, mobility decisions.
Layer 2MAC, RLC, PDCP: scheduling grants, HARQ, segmentation, ciphering, header compression.
Baseband PHYModulation, coding, FFT, MIMO detection, channel estimation, synchronization.
RF transceiverADC and DAC, mixers, PLLs, filters. Moves between baseband and carrier frequency.
RF front endPower amplifier, LNA, switches, filters, duplexers, antenna tuners.
AntennasSeveral per band; arrays and beams for millimeter wave.
Digital: software, DSP, fixed-function acceleratorsAnalog and RF: filters, amplifiers, antennas
Also insideSIM or eSIM and key handling, clocks, power management interface, GNSS coexistence, and test and calibration hooks.
The permanent trade-offThroughput against power, silicon area, heat and cost, across dozens of bands and up to five generations at once.

The channel sets the ceiling

Multipath fading, Doppler, noise and interference make the radio channel hostile. Shannon gives the ideal upper bound on what any modem can get out of it.

BandwidthCosts spectrum and RF and baseband power. Doubling it doubles capacity.
SNRCosts link budget and interference control. At high SNR each 6 dB adds about 1 bit per hertz per layer.
Spatial layersMIMO multiplies capacity when the channel has rank. Costs antennas, compute and power.
ideal upper bound, C = layers × B × log2(1 + SNR). Real modems reach a fraction of it.
One transmitted signal arrives as several copies with different delays (multipath) and a frequency shift that depends on speed (Doppler).

2G: digital voice, shared by time

GSM digitized voice and let eight users take turns on each 200 kHz carrier. Time slots separate users; different carriers separate cells.

Access TDMA + FDMACarrier 200 kHzModulation GMSK, 8PSK in EDGEData 9.6 kbps circuit; GPRS up to ~171 kbps; EDGE up to ~384 kbps
What the modem paysEqualizer (Viterbi / MLSE) against multipath, frequency hopping, timing advance. Small DSP, tiny power budget.
Say it: 2G taught the core timing lesson. Users far from the tower must transmit early (timing advance) so their slot lands in the right place.
Each slot lasts about 577 µs. The highlighted slot belongs to one phone; it transmits a burst once per frame.

3G: everyone talks at once, separated by codes

UMTS spread every user across the full 5 MHz. Unique spreading codes keep them apart, so interference, not time slots, becomes the capacity limit.

Access WCDMACarrier 5 MHz, 3.84 McpsR99 384 kbps typicalHSDPA ~14 Mbps, 16QAM, HARQ, 2 ms TTIHSPA+ 42 Mbps+, 64QAM, MIMO, dual carrier
What the modem paysRake receiver to combine multipath, closed-loop power control at 1500 Hz, soft handover, chip-rate processing, 2G fallback. Multi-mode begins here.
Say it: CDMA trades frequency planning for power control. The near-far problem is why every phone must transmit only as loud as needed.
Receivers recover one user by correlating with that user's code; the others look like noise.

4G: many narrow subcarriers, one flat channel each

LTE splits the band into 15 kHz subcarriers that are orthogonal. Each sees a flat channel, so one complex multiply per subcarrier equalizes it. A cyclic prefix absorbs delay spread.

Downlink OFDMAUplink SC-FDMACarrier up to 20 MHzTTI 1 msMIMO up to 4×4, 256QAM, turbo codeLTE-A carrier aggregation
What the modem paysFFT engines, MIMO detection, turbo decoder throughput, dozens of bands, carrier-aggregation band combinations in the RF, IMS for VoLTE.
Say it: uplink uses SC-FDMA because a lower peak-to-average power ratio lets the phone's amplifier run efficiently. That is battery and coverage.
Each curve is one subcarrier. At every subcarrier's peak, all the others are exactly zero, so they do not interfere.

5G NR: flexible numerology, beams, two spectrum worlds

NR keeps OFDM but makes it configurable. Subcarrier spacing scales, slots shrink for low latency, and millimeter wave adds beamforming as a core modem job.

FR1 up to 100 MHz carriersFR2 24.25–52.6 GHz, up to 400 MHzCoding LDPC data, Polar controlModes NSA (EN-DC) and SAIMT-2020 20 Gbps, ~1 ms
What the modem paysBeam management, wide-bandwidth ADCs and FFT, LDPC throughput, two radios active under EN-DC, mmWave heat, UE power-saving features.
Say it: subcarrier spacing = 15 × 2μ kHz, slot = 1 / 2μ ms. Higher μ is for millimeter wave and low latency.

Each generation bought roughly 10 to 50 times the peak rate

Peak rates are specification targets under ideal conditions. They show direction and ratio, not what a user sees.

Peak data rate, log scale. 2G is EDGE, 3G is HSPA+ (R8), 4G is LTE-Advanced, 5G is the IMT-2020 target, 6G is the top of the IMT-2030 range (50 to 200 Gbps by scenario).
Single-carrier bandwidth, log scale. LTE aggregates carriers beyond 20 MHz; NR FR2 reaches 400 MHz per carrier.
Say it: rate = bandwidth × spectral efficiency × layers. Look at which factor each generation pulled: 3G bandwidth, 4G spectral efficiency and layers, 5G bandwidth again.

Patterns to internalize

  • Each generation raises bandwidth and spectral efficiency; the modem pays in compute and power.
  • Intelligence moves toward the edge: fast scheduling and HARQ in 3G, beam management in 5G, learned PHY functions next.
  • Backward compatibility is a tax: a 6G phone modem will be five-mode.
  • New spectrum forces new RF architecture and coexistence work.
  • In a phone, power and heat limit throughput long before the standard does.

The clock to 6G

3GPP is using two releases: Release 20 for studies, Release 21 for the first normative 6G work. Commercial service is widely expected around 2030.

The 6G air interface looks a lot like 5G

The headline from the first agreements: the physical layer is evolutionary. The big changes are in system design, AI, sensing and spectrum.

Settled or agreed Sourced

  • CP-OFDM in the downlink; CP-OFDM and DFT-s-OFDM in the uplink. DFT-s-OFDM gives better phone coverage.
  • 5G channel codes (LDPC and Polar) largely reused, with possible extensions.
  • A new radio access technology with standalone operation, not backward compatible with NR (called 6GR in the study).
  • Avoid defining several numerologies for the same frequency band.
  • AI/ML must have a non-AI fallback so service holds when AI features are absent.
  • Sensing is studied as a native air-interface capability: waveform, reference signals, measurement feedback.

Still open or to verify Analysis

  • Modulation details, reference signal structure and supported bandwidths: the milestone list names them, so read the outcome in the RAN1 study report (TR 38.760-1).
  • Which AI/ML use cases become normative and how models are managed across vendors.
  • How sensing coexists with communication in a phone modem.
  • RAN and core functional split, and how NTN fits natively.
  • Security framework beyond control-message protection.

Key documents: TR 38.914 (scenarios and requirements), TR 38.760-1 (6G radio, RAN1), TS 22.270 (system requirements, draft), TR 22.870 (use cases), TR 23.801-01 (architecture).

Say it: if asked what changes in the modem, do not start with a new waveform. Start with wider spectrum, AI with fallback, sensing, NTN and energy per bit.

Where 6G is looking for spectrum

Log frequency axis. WRC-27 agenda item 1.7 studies 4.4–4.8, 7.125–8.4 and 14.8–15.35 GHz; WRC-23 identified upper 6 GHz (6.425–7.125) for IMT in some regions. Sourced
Why the 7 to 15 GHz rangeMore than 2 GHz of possible spectrum with propagation closer to bands below 7 GHz than to millimeter wave, so existing sites can be reused. The costs: weaker uplink coverage and larger arrays.
It is not settledRegulators differ: CEPT and the UK have been skeptical of the case for more spectrum, while the US is pushing 7 and 8 GHz. Outcomes come at WRC-27 in late 2027.

Formulas you should be able to derive

Shannon capacity

C = B · log₂(1 + SNR)

MIMO: about min(Nt, Nr) layers × this, when the channel has rank. 100 MHz at 20 dB: 100 × 6.66 = 666 Mbps per layer.

OFDM timing

Tsym = 1 / Δf, Δf = 15 · 2μ kHz

15 kHz → 66.7 µs useful symbol. Cyclic prefix must exceed delay spread.

Doppler

fd = v · fc / c

500 km/h at 7 GHz: 138.9 m/s × 7 GHz / c ≈ 3.24 kHz, about 22% of a 15 kHz subcarrier.

Free-space path loss

FSPL = 20 log₁₀(4π d f / c)

Doubling frequency adds 6 dB between isotropic antennas. Same-area arrays recover it with beamforming gain.

Noise floor and sensitivity

N = −174 dBm/Hz + 10 log₁₀(B) + NF

20 MHz, NF 7 dB: −174 + 73 + 7 = −94 dBm. Sensitivity = N + required SNR.

Subcarriers and sampling

fs = NFFT · Δf

LTE 20 MHz: 2048 × 15 kHz = 30.72 MHz. Check your FFT size against the carrier before sizing the ADC.
